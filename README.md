## Course Overview

SHM 377 examines the management of hazardous materials, including the
scientific principles, professional practices, and regulatory frameworks
associated with their transportation, storage, use, exposure, release,
emergency response, and disposal.

The course emphasizes applied problem-solving rather than memorization.

Students use scientific information, quantitative methods, workplace and
environmental data, regulatory resources, and case studies to evaluate
hazardous-material problems and develop evidence-based management decisions.

---

## Course Learning Outcomes

Students completing SHM 377 should be able to:

1. Anticipate, recognize, evaluate, and develop control strategies associated
   with hazardous materials.
2. Utilize federal regulations related to hazardous materials to assess
   compliance.
3. Utilize Department of Transportation regulations related to hazardous
   materials to assess compliance.

## Course Approach

Hazardous-material management requires more than identifying a chemical or
memorizing a regulation.

Professionals must determine what material is present, understand how it
behaves, evaluate how it can move through workplace or environmental systems,
determine how it can be measured, interpret available data, identify
applicable requirements, and make defensible management decisions.

A recurring reasoning framework throughout SHM 377 is:

```text
Material
   ↓
Phase / Form
   ↓
Medium
   ↓
Movement
   ↓
Properties
   ↓
Measurement
   ↓
Data
   ↓
Decision
```

Students progressively apply this framework to workplace and environmental
hazardous-material problems throughout the quarter.

## Chemistry and Quantitative Reasoning

Chemistry is used as a tool for understanding hazardous-material behavior
rather than treated as a separate general chemistry course.

Students apply concepts such as:

- Density
- Specific gravity
- Mass and volume
- Concentration
- Unit conversions
- ppm
- Molarity
- Volatility
- Solubility
- Vapor pressure
- Chemical compatibility
- Reactivity

Calculations are introduced in the context of hazardous-material problems and
management decisions.

---

## Regulations

Regulations are treated as professional resources rather than lists of facts
to memorize.

Students practice locating, navigating, interpreting, and applying regulatory
and technical information to hazardous-material scenarios.

Regulatory frameworks examined in the course may include requirements
administered by:

- Occupational Safety and Health Administration (OSHA)
- U.S. Department of Transportation (DOT)
- U.S. Environmental Protection Agency (EPA)
- Other applicable federal and state agencies

Students should develop the ability to determine which requirements apply,
locate the relevant information, evaluate compliance, and recommend
appropriate action.

## R and RStudio

SHM 377 uses **R and RStudio** as tools for analyzing and communicating
hazardous-material and exposure data.

Students are not expected to enter the course already knowing R, and the
course is not intended to be a programming course.

Early laboratory activities provide substantial guidance and reusable code.
As the quarter progresses, students gradually take greater responsibility for
selecting, modifying, interpreting, and communicating their analyses.

Students may use R to:

- Import and inspect datasets
- Organize and clean data
- Perform calculations
- Calculate descriptive statistics
- Create tables
- Visualize measurements
- Compare observations
- Identify patterns
- Interpret evidence

The emphasis is on using data to answer hazardous-material management
questions.

## Laboratories

Laboratory activities connect hazardous-material concepts with measurement,
data analysis, regulatory research, and management decisions.

A typical laboratory may ask students to:

```text
Observe a problem
        ↓
Understand how measurements were collected
        ↓
Work with a dataset
        ↓
Analyze the data
        ↓
Interpret the results
        ↓
Locate relevant technical or regulatory information
        ↓
Develop a management recommendation
        ↓
Communicate and defend the decision
```

Laboratories may use photographs, videos, process diagrams, public datasets,
and instructor-created scenarios to approximate the reasoning involved in
real hazardous-material investigations.

When simulated datasets are used, they will be identified as simulated.

## Final Project

The final project will provide students with an opportunity to integrate the
major skills developed throughout SHM 377.

Students will investigate a hazardous-material problem or case and develop an
evidence-based analysis that may incorporate:

- Hazard characterization
- Material behavior
- Measurement data
- Quantitative analysis
- R-generated figures and tables
- Technical resources
- Regulatory interpretation
- Management recommendations
- Professional communication

The final project structure and requirements will be provided separately as
the course progresses.
